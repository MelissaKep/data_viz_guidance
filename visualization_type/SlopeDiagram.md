# Slope Diagram

Also known as: slope chart, slopegraph

Slope diagrams show levels and changes in values between two time points and represent the simplest form of a line plot. They are most often used to show the differences in levels and changes of a variable across different categorical groups. They are also a useful illustration of changes in rank between two time points and provide more information than the rank-specific bump chart. 

The figure consists of two vertical axes representing the two time points. The points for each group are plotted on each axis and then a line connects the two points for each group. 

Slope diagrams may be used when only two data points are available or when the start and end data points of a longer series are all that are necessary to illustrate important patterns. 

Those using slope diagrams should carefully consider whether absolute or relative change in values is of most interest. Slope charts, especially with small values, can over-emphasize absolute change and mask important differences in relative change.


## Guidance

In addition to the guidance provided in this section, relevant guidance can also be found in the Agency Logo, Axes, Colors, Grids, Legends, Source, Titles, Typography sections.

### Essential

* Directly label all lines in static slope diagrams. 

## Additional Recommendations

### Recommended

*	Limit the number of lines included in a static slope diagram to 15, especially in cases with many overlapping lines. 
*	Incorporate flags to denote when slopes are statistically significant.
*	If more lines are necessary, consider building an interactive slope diagram where individual lines can be selected and details about the selected line can be provided.

### Not Recommended

*	Do not use slope diagrams when the goal is to highlight relative change. 
*	Do not use slope diagrams when trends between the two time points are known to be highly variable or when reducing the data to two time points masks important trends in the data

 ***Example 2:*** Percentage with Health Insurance Coverage for Adults 19 to 64 Years, 2021-2022

While the majority of slopes are not steep, the asterisk denoting statistical significance helps users better interpret the visualization and changes across time. However, each small multiple uses a different y-scale. This makes it very hard to compare across the plots. As stated in the small multiple standards, each panel should have the same axis scales.
