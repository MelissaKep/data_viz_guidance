# Population Pyramid

Also known as: age-sex pyramid

A population pyramid is a graphical illustration of the age distribution in a population by sex. Either percentages or counts can be displayed.

Population pyramids consist of a series of histogram bars on opposing sides of the y-axis, with one side representing males and the other females. Each row (or bin) of a population pyramid represents an age or age group and together they form a general shape of the age and sex distribution. Historically, populations have had a larger number of younger people than older people, resulting in the pyramid shape. However, the age distribution of many modern populations may not appear pyramid shaped.

## Best Practices 

### Essential 

* Place the y-axis in the middle of the x-axis at the zero marker. On the x-axis, the dividing point of the y-axis between the male bars and the female bars must be at 0.
* Display males on the left and females on the right.
* Display age groups on the y-axis and population size on the x-axis.
* Use uniform bin sizes (except for the oldest age category). 
* If displaying percentages, the denominator for the percentages should be the total population, not the female or male population. In other words, the sum of all bars must add up to 100%, not 200%.
* Use the same scale for the both the female and male x-axis. 
* If showing side-by-side or small multiple population pyramids, use the same scale for all population pyramids (Example 1).
* Order ages or age groups in descending order with oldest ages at the top and youngest ages at the bottom.

## Additional Recommendations 

### Recommended  

* Use one-year or five-year age groups. Larger age groupings will decrease the granularity of the population pyramid and another chart type may be better.
* Leave space between each row or add a border to each bar.
* Use contrasting color to distinguish groups in the population pyramid. This may include:
   * Using one color to represent males and a different color to represent females (Example 2).
   * Using color to highlight certain age groups (Example 3).
   * Using color to show superimposed population pyramids (Example 4).


### Not Recommended  

* Using more than two colors can make the interpretation of the shape of the distribution much more cognitively taxing for the viewer (Example 5).
* Do not alternate between two shades of color for each row for improved legibility.
