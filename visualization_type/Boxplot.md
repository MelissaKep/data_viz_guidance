# Boxplot

Also known as: box and whisker plot

A boxplot is a graph that presents a simplified distribution of a continuous variable. Boxplots are particularly useful for comparing distributions across different groups that can be difficult to represent with other visualizations such as histograms. Visual representations of distributions are useful for identifying skewed data and the presence of outliers. 

The most basic form of the boxplot presents five points of data: minimum, first quartile (Q1), median, third quartile (Q3), and maximum. The box is bounded by the first and third quartiles with the median marked as a line within the box; the “whiskers” of the plot represent the minimum and maximum values. 

Alternative forms of the boxplot define the “whiskers” differently, such as 1.5 times the inter-quartile range or IQR (the distance between the first and third quartiles). Any observations beyond the “whiskers” are considered outliers. Outliers may be omitted from the figure or plotted as individual data points beyond the “whiskers.”

Boxplots can be displayed horizontally or vertically and displayed side-by-side for comparisons.

## Guidance

In addition to the guidance provided in this section, relevant guidance can also be found in the Agency Logo, Axes, Colors, Grids, Legends, Source, Titles, Typography sections.

### Essential

* Clearly describe each component of the boxplot, either through direct labels or in the figure notes. For instance, specify whether the “whiskers” represent minimums and maximums or whether they are defined in some other way. 
* State whether outliers are omitted.

### Avoid

* Do not use minimum and maximum values for “whiskers” when data are heavily skewed (e.g., a “whisker” is much larger than the box component of the boxplot).
* Do not use boxplots when comparing large changes or differences in data values.

## Additional Recommendations

* Use boxplot as an indicator of centrality, symmetry, or spread in the data.
* Use parallel boxplots when comparing multiple samples that would be impossible for bar charts.
