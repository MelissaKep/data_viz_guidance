# Stacked Area Chart

Also known as:  overlapping area chart, percent stacked area chart, 100% stacked area chart

Stacked area charts are a type of area chart that used to show how several quantities combine to make a whole across time. In a stacked area chart, series that are parts of a whole are displayed on top of each other. The space between the series line and the next series is filled with some color. In stacked area plots, the y-axis can either be the continuous number to show the total number/value or be bounded by 0% to 100% to show percentage of the total.  the y-axis is typically a continuous variable but can also be ordinal. In either case, data on the y- axis must allow for 0.

A stacked area chart is useful when the purpose is to track the total value and/or the breakdown of the total by groups. The shading allows for comparing the heights of each shaded curve to see of how each subgroup compares to the others in their contributions to the total.

## Guidance

In addition to the guidance provided in this section, relevant guidance can also be found in the Agency Logo, Axes, Colors, Grids, Legends, Source, Titles, Typography sections.

### Essential

* The y-axis must start at 0.
* High contrast colors/greyscale should always be used—see the categorical color guidance.

### Avoid

* Do not use area charts where the dimension being measured has no relationship to 0.

## Additional Recommendations

### Recommended

* The largest or most stable groups should be at the bottom, with the most variable or smallest groups on top.
* Consider collapsing series together to limit the number of series so that the main trends become more apparent.

### Not Recommended

* Stacked area plots are not recommended for showing volatile data over times or when differences are very small.
