# Notes 

Visualizations should be self-explanatory, even in cases where the visualization is embedded in a larger document (e.g. working papers). In practice, this means that the major point(s) could still be understood if the visualization independent from the rest of the document. If you require the reader to go back and forth between the text and the figure, you are asking the reader to put in extra effort. Notes and captions help prevent this, in addition to clear titles, subtitles, and axes.  They can be preceded with “Notes:”, “Caption:”, “Figure”, etc.

The primary purposes of notes and captions are:

*	Clarity: They help to clarify the meaning and intent of the visual.
*	Context: They provide context and background information that may be necessary for understanding the visual
*	Emphasis: They can be used to emphasize important points or findings.

A note is used to provide additional context. Notes can define terms and symbols used in the visualization, provide information on what comparisons are statistically insignificant, or offer insights that are not immediately apparent from the visual alone. Notes are placed within the frame of the visualization and below the graphics. 

A note is used to provide additional context. Notes can define terms and symbols outside of legends used in the visualization, provide information about universes and sampling, provide information on what comparisons are statistically insignificant, etc. Notes are placed within the frame of the visualization and below the graphics and are preceded with “Notes:”.

In some publications, a caption is used to provide a concise summary of the visual's content and offer insights that are not immediately apparent from the visual alone. It should be informative and easy to understand, even for those who are not familiar with the subject matter. Captions are placed directly below the visual outside the frame. 


## Guidance  

## Best Practices 

### Essential 

*	In a static visualization, notes should be at the bottom either within the frame of the visualization or adjacent to the graphic. Only source statements should be under the notes. Captions go outside the frame of the visualization and adjacent to the visualization.
*	In an interactive visualization, notes should be at the bottom of a graphic or provided as a pop-up. If provided by selecting a button, the button should be clear that more information is available via an icon.

## Additional Recommendations 

### Recommended  

* Notes should define all jargon, define all symbols not included in a legend, state the universe if not included elsewhere (e.g., title, legend, axis), and provide any other details needed for the user to accurately make conclusions from the visualization.
