# Typography

Typography is one of the most effective tools for communicating the meaning of data to users. When building data visualizations, establishing a clear hierarchy of typographic elements and using easy-to-read font families will help users better understand and interact with your data.  By carefully selecting fonts, adjusting font sizes, and using techniques like bolding, italics, and color variations, designers can emphasize key data points, differentiate between categories, and ensure that the visualization effectively communicates its intended message.
Typography is especially important in the digital age. Viewing a computer or digital screen often makes the eyes work harder. Viewing a computer or digital screen is different from reading a printed page. Often the letters on the computer or handheld device are not as precise or sharply defined, the level of contrast of the letters to the background is reduced, and the presence of glare and reflections on the screen may make viewing difficult. To help reduce digital eye strain, designers must carefully choose color and fonts and minimize extraneous and distracting clutter.
For typography specifically related to mobile design, please see [Components: Mobile Design page](mobiledesign.html).

## Best Practices 

### Essential 

* The PIO standard font is Gotham. Gotham is not available in all software (e.g. Tableau). Acceptable alternative fonts are listed below: 
   * Calibri
   * Cambria
   * Tableau Book
   * Verdana
   * Georgia
   * Gisha 
* Use a text size of at least 8 points (pt).
* Use labels that are easily readable without interfering with the content, especially in maps.
* For interactive visualizations with multiple pages, use consistent font from page to page.
* Avoid overly thin or light fonts that may be difficult to read. For more guidance on this, see guidance related to contrast ratio for [508 accessibility and compliance](https://uscensus.sharepoint.com/sites/508E/Pages/PDFs.aspx).
