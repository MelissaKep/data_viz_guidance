# Titles and subtitles

Titles and subtitles are important components to any data visualization. As titles and subtitles are typically the very first thing the eye sees, it sets the stage and tone for your visualization. 

Titles give users a clear overview of what a data visualization is about. A title should broadly describe the main points of a data visualization without identifying any causes.

Subtitles can be used to describe any trends or highlights in a data visualization. Subtitles can also be used to indicate the unit of measurement if preferable to an axis title. While subtitles are optional, their presence helps to clarify the purpose of a visualization to users.


## Guidance  

## Best Practices 

### Essential 

* All visualizations need to have a title.
* Titles should appear above a visualization or as the first element directly adjacent to it.
* Subtitles should appear directly below the title.



### Avoid

      
 
## Additional Recommendations 

### Recommended  

* Use caution when titles and subtitles are more than two lines each. 
* Titles and subtitles can be used to specify the unit of analysis. Depending on the visualization, this information may be better suited for the axis labels.
* Do not use symbols in titles and subtitles for example “State & Local Governments” should be “State and Local Governments.”  
* Check legacy visualization to confirm uniqueness of title. (Subtitle can help differentiate.)

### Not Recommended  


## Examples 

***Example 1:*** Not good Title 

### Lower fertility rates have increased the average age in the US for the past decade

Why is not good: The title is too long and includes a causal statement rather than the data visualization’s main point: the trend that average age has increased in the US. 


***Example 2:*** Acceptable Title

### Age in the United States

Why is this only acceptable? While the title is short, it is vague and doesn’t reveal much context about what the chart is trying to convey. This title could be paired with a subtitle to contextualize the data visualization more.


***Example 3:*** Better Title and subtitle

### Age in the United States 
#### Older adults are projected to outnumber children by 2035.

Why is this Better? Now that the title has been paired with a subtitle, users will better understand what the data visualization is trying to communicate.
